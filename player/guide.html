Everything on Earth glows in infrared heat. PREFIRE splits that glow into 63 channels, like 63 very narrow colours from 1 to 53 µm. Each channel effectively reports the temperature of whatever it can see. Some wavelengths pass straight through the air and see the ground. Others are absorbed by CO₂ or water vapour, so all they see is cold air high up. The player gives each channel its own tone. A warmer view makes the tone louder.

3. Wavelength → pitch

In Spectral chorus mode, shorter wavelengths play higher. Each dot below is one channel at its pitch (default range 90–2400 Hz).

Where each channel sits in pitch

chorus mode, default range
Sound modeOne channel becomes…Listen for
Spectral chorusA steady sine tone at a fixed pitch (above). Its volume follows the channel. Chords thinning or filling in. Over cloud, the middle register (window, ~280–400 Hz) fades while the bass holds. At the poles, the whole chord goes quiet.
Harmonic seriesOne overtone of a single note. The longest wavelength is the fundamental. Timbre, not melody. The root note rises as the scene warms (window temperature), and the tone colour changes with the spectrum's shape.
Pentatonic plucksA note that only sounds when the channel changes quickly. Edges: coastlines, cloud boundaries, the ice edge. A high pluck means warming; a lower, softer one means cooling. Silence means nothing is changing.

Things to spot: whole columns go dark at the poles (everything is cold). In the tropics, dark streaks appear only in the middle rows; those are cloud tops blocking the window. The CO₂ rows (around 15 µm) change slowly and smoothly, because they see the upper air, not the clouds. They're the slow drift under everything. The Sunlight rows switch off on the night side, and the thin dark line through the far-infrared is a masked detector, which stays silent.
